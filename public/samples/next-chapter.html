Six-week career coaching

Make your next career move with a plan.

Work through your options, sharpen your story and leave each session knowing what to do next.

Request an introduction call

A place to work through your next move.

You have experience. You also have questions about what comes next. Coaching gives you time to examine those questions and turn them into decisions you can act on.

“I know I need a change.”

Separate what is wrong with your current role from what you want in your next one.

“I have too many options.”

Compare paths against your priorities, commitments and the kind of work you want to do.

“I don't know how to explain my experience.”

Build a clear story for applications and conversations, using examples from your own work.

Six weeks.
Space to think, then act.

Individual sessions, practical exercises and a plan you revise as you learn. Your pace and priorities shape the work.

  1. Weeks 1–2: decide what matters

    Look at the work you enjoy, the conditions you need and the tradeoffs you are willing to make.

  2. Weeks 3–4: explore and position

    Choose paths to investigate. Turn your experience into a story that fits those opportunities.

  3. Weeks 5–6: plan your next steps

    Set a realistic search rhythm, prepare for conversations and decide how to review progress.

Something useful between sessions.

The work continues in small decisions. A planning worksheet helps you turn an open question into one action for the week.

Before we work together.

The introduction is a chance to ask questions and see whether the format suits you.

Do I need to know the career I want?

No. Sorting through possible directions can be part of the program. You do need to be willing to explore and do the work between sessions.

Will I get a job in six weeks?

The six weeks describe the coaching program. Hiring timelines depend on employers and your situation; a job offer is not guaranteed.

How much time should I make available?

Allow time for a weekly session and the actions you agree between sessions. The introduction call helps set expectations around your schedule.

Is this therapy or recruitment?

This concept describes career planning and coaching. It does not provide clinical support or act as a recruitment agency.

What would you like to change?

You can start with the part you are sure about, even if the next role is still a question.
