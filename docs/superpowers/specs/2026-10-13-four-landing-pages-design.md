# Four portfolio landing pages: requirements and brand brief

Status: requirements complete; visual design and implementation remain next phases.

## Purpose

Replace six placeholder website entries with four complete portfolio landing pages for agencies, coaches, service businesses and ecommerce. A potential client should be able to inspect the work and conclude that Philip understands their customers and can build a useful website for their business.

These are fictional brand concepts with finished copy and imagery. They demonstrate realistic customer experiences. A discreet portfolio-preview label identifies the concepts, and any completed form or checkout clearly states that it is a demonstration.

## Scope

- Four standalone landing pages, accessible through the existing Projects website preview.
- Each has its own brand identity, content sequence and imagery. The portfolio shell keeps its existing green identity; the sample brands may use different palettes.
- One primary conversion action per page, repeated where useful.
- Working local interactions, accessible forms, FAQ disclosures and clear success states.
- No accounts, live payments, email delivery, CRM integration or backend required.
- Reduce the visible website collection to four during implementation. Preserve unused sample files until references have been checked; removal is not necessary to complete this work.
- Refresh the barrel thumbnails and homepage project previews to show the finished pages.

## 1. Relay Studio: agency

Audience: owners of small service businesses whose inquiries arrive across forms, email and messages.

Offer: a focused setup of a landing page, CRM pipeline and follow-up sequence, with a handover the team can use.

Hero headline: **Give every inquiry a clear next step.**

Supporting copy: Relay connects your website, CRM and follow-up so your team can see who needs a reply and what happens next.

Primary action: **Book a systems review**. It opens a short inline request form with name, work email and the current inquiry bottleneck. Submission produces a demo request summary; it does not claim a real appointment is booked.

Page sequence: offer and primary action; an example inquiry journey; what the setup includes; delivery process; who it fits; FAQs; request form.

Proposed visual direction: confident editorial layout, ink and warm ivory with a restrained cobalt accent, large typography and a prominent annotated workflow artifact. Avoid presenting fabricated analytics as results.

Images: one purposeful team/workshop photograph and a sample website-to-CRM visual built from actual page content. The workflow artifact is the main proof of understanding.

## 2. Next Chapter: coaching

Audience: experienced professionals considering a career change who need help choosing a direction and preparing their next move.

Offer: a six-week coaching program covering priorities, career options, positioning and an actionable search plan. Program length describes the service, not a guaranteed outcome.

Hero headline: **Make your next career move with a plan.**

Supporting copy: Work through your options, sharpen your story and leave each session knowing what to do next.

Primary action: **Request an introduction call**. Form fields: name, email and what the visitor wants to change. Demo completion explains that no request was sent.

Page sequence: personal introduction and offer; common decision points; six-week program outline; example planning worksheet; coach approach; fit and expectations; FAQs; introduction form.

Proposed visual direction: warm editorial portrait, oat and plum palette, serif display type paired with a readable sans serif, generous space and conversational copy.

Images: consistent fictional coach portrait plus a natural working-session scene. Do not claim qualifications or use a generated person as a real customer endorsement.

## 3. Sunday Home: service business

Audience: busy households looking for a dependable regular or one-off home cleaning service.

Offer: clearly scoped home cleaning with a straightforward quote request. The page explains inclusions, preparation and the next step.

Hero headline: **Come home to a clean space.**

Supporting copy: Choose the clean your home needs, tell us about your space and get a clear estimate before you book.

Primary action: **Get my cleaning quote**. A compact form collects cleaning type, bedroom count, area and email. It displays the supplied information and a demo acknowledgment, without calculating an unsupported price or promising availability.

Page sequence: inviting home photograph and quote action; cleaning options; inclusions and exclusions; how a visit works; preparation checklist; service-area information; FAQs; quote form.

Proposed visual direction: bright domestic photography, soft blue and deep navy with a warm yellow accent, clear service lists and practical controls.

Images: lived-in clean interior, cleaning detail and a team member at work. Keep these scenes consistent; avoid staged before/after claims and invented ratings.

## 4. Fieldwork: ecommerce

Audience: commuters and occasional travelers looking for one practical everyday backpack.

Offer: the fictional Fieldwork Daypack, 22 L capacity, padded compartment fitting laptops up to 16 inches, two colors (Graphite and Sand), illustrative price US$89.

Hero headline: **One bag for the day ahead.**

Supporting copy: A place for your laptop, daily essentials and the extra layer you take everywhere.

Primary action: **Add to bag**. Color choice, quantity (1–5), gallery and cart summary work locally. Checkout ends in an explicit demo state, with no payment fields or purchase confirmation.

Page sequence: product photography with purchasing controls; compartments and use details; material and dimensions; commute/travel scenes; delivery and return policy examples identified as concept policies; FAQs; repeat purchase action.

Proposed visual direction: product-led composition, bone and charcoal with muted orange accents, restrained typography and large photography. Price and selected color remain visible beside the action.

Images: consistent product shape across Graphite and Sand studio shots, interior/compartment detail and one in-use photograph. Do not claim waterproofing, certifications, sustainability or warranty evidence that has not been supplied.

## Shared UX requirements

- Visitors understand what is offered and the primary action in the first viewport at desktop and mobile widths.
- Short brand navigation uses section anchors; the portfolio preview provides a predictable back/close affordance outside the brand page.
- Images are locally stored, sized appropriately, have reserved dimensions and meaningful alt text where informative. Decorative images have empty alt text. Below-fold images load lazily.
- Forms have visible labels, useful validation near the affected field, preserved input after errors, keyboard operation and announced completion feedback.
- No fake countdowns, scarcity claims, customer logos, testimonials, star ratings or performance percentages.
- FAQs answer the actual purchase or inquiry questions for each offer.
- Support 320 px mobile, tablet and desktop layouts, 200% zoom, visible focus and reduced motion. Target WCAG AA contrast.
- Light/dark variants retain each brand's character and keep product photographs natural; avoid using blend filters to obscure image content.
- Motion supports interaction and orientation. Avoid mandatory scroll effects, autoplay carousels and constant decorative animations.

## Design and copy review

Use UI/UX Pro Max to check patterns, accessibility and typography recommendations, then judge the fit for each brand. Taste Skill and Impeccable guide composition, visual identity and critique. Humanizer and No AI Slop review the completed copy for filler, generic phrases and unsupported claims. Ponytail keeps implementation proportional to the portfolio scope. Agent Reach is available when further public reference research is needed.

The four pages must differ in image direction, content structure, typography and interaction emphasis. Shared code is acceptable; identical-looking templates with changed colors are not the intended result.

## Acceptance and delivery sequence

1. Requirements: this brief fixes the brands, offers, page content and behavior.
2. Design: create desktop/mobile concepts and an image plan for each brand. Start with Relay Studio so the first page establishes the quality bar.
3. Implementation: build one complete page at a time, then update the four-entry registry and project thumbnails. Inspect existing preview and iframe behavior before choosing the implementation structure.
4. Testing: verify each page's primary action, validation, reset/reload behavior, image loading, mobile layout and navigation from Projects. Run the existing regression suite after integration.
5. Review: inspect all four pages in both themes and representative widths. Fix the observed issues in a bounded pass.
6. Deployment: publish only when separately requested.

Requirements work does not modify the current website or remove the existing placeholders.
