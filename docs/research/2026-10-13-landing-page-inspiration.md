# Landing Page Inspiration Research

## Purpose

Inform the design specification for four fictional portfolio landing pages: an agency, a coach, a service business, and an ecommerce brand.

## Findings

### Offer-first positioning

Acquisition.com presents Alex Hormozi's framework around a clear offer, customer demand, and the value the customer wants. For this portfolio, that means each page should lead with one audience, one painful problem, one believable outcome, and one offer instead of presenting a general list of services.

Source: [Acquisition.com — About Alex Hormozi](https://www.acquisition.com/about-alex-hormozi)

### Single conversion goal

Unbounce recommends a clear call to action, message match between the visitor's source and the page, reduced navigation, authentic proof, strong visuals, and mobile-first layout. Each portfolio landing page should therefore have one primary CTA and repeat that CTA at useful decision points.

Sources: [Unbounce landing page best practices](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-articles/landing-page-best-practices/), [Unbounce landing page documentation](https://documentation.unbounce.com/hc/en-us/articles/360061988151-What-Are-Some-Best-Practices-When-Designing-My-Landing-Page)

### Ecommerce conversion content

Shopify recommends a clear value proposition, high-quality product images, benefit-led headlines, social proof, trust signals, FAQs, responsive layout, and reduced navigation for ecommerce landing pages. The ecommerce concept should show the product in use, explain why it is different, answer purchase objections, and make the buy action obvious.

Sources: [Shopify ecommerce landing page guidance](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ecommerce-landing-page), [Shopify landing page best practices](https://www.shopify.com/blog/landing-page)

## Design implications for this project

- Keep all four pages in the portfolio's green botanical visual world, but give each fictional brand a distinct accent color, typography treatment, image direction, and offer.
- Use real-looking but clearly fictional sample content. Do not claim real clients, revenue, conversion rates, or testimonials.
- Use generated or local visual assets only when they explain the offer. Do not use decorative stock imagery without a job.
- Build each page as a self-contained landing page with focused navigation and a single primary CTA.
- Treat mobile as a primary layout, since landing-page traffic is commonly mobile-heavy.
- Keep the four page structures related enough to demonstrate reusable frontend systems, but distinct enough to show design judgment.

## Candidate page directions

1. Agency: a growth systems agency selling a qualified-lead and follow-up system. Primary CTA: book a systems audit.
2. Coach: a premium coaching program selling a structured transformation with an application CTA. Primary CTA: apply for a clarity call.
3. Service business: a local or regional service business selling a fast, trusted booking experience. Primary CTA: request an estimate or book service.
4. Ecommerce: a focused product brand selling one hero product or product bundle. Primary CTA: shop the product.

## Scope boundary

This research supports planning only. It does not authorize copying any referenced website, importing claims, or implementing pages before the design specification is approved.
